🔬 The Science Behind Tsunamis

Understanding how these powerful ocean waves are formed.

A tsunami is not a single wave, but a series of powerful waves caused by a large-scale displacement of a body of water. The most common cause is a powerful underwater earthquake, which vertically lifts or drops the seabed, displacing a massive column of water. This creates an initial wave that can travel across entire oceans.

From Open Ocean to Shore

In the deep ocean, a tsunami wave has a long wavelength and a very small height, making it barely noticeable. As it approaches the shallow coastal waters, the wave slows down, but its height dramatically increases. This is why a tsunami wave can appear as a towering wall of water or a fast-moving, powerful surge.
