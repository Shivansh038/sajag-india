🔬 The Science Behind Cyclones

Understanding how these powerful storms form and behave.

A cyclone, also known as a hurricane or typhoon, is a massive, rotating storm system that forms over warm ocean waters. These storms are essentially giant heat engines, powered by the evaporation of warm sea surface water. As warm, moist air rises, it creates a low-pressure area, causing more air to rush in. This air begins to spin due to the Earth's rotation, forming the characteristic vortex.

Key Features

  • The Eye: The calm, clear center of the storm where the air is sinking.
  • The Eyewall: The ring of thunderstorms immediately surrounding the eye, where the most intense winds and heaviest rainfall occur.
  • Rainbands: Bands of thunderstorms extending outwards from the eyewall, bringing heavy rain and winds.
