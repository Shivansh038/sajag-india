Understanding Floods

How they happen and how to stay safe.

1. What is a Flood?

A flood happens when water overflows onto land that is usually dry. It's basically too much water in the wrong place at the wrong time.

2. How Floods Happen

Floods are caused by several factors, often acting together:

  • Heavy Rainfall: When rain falls faster than the ground or rivers can absorb it, water builds up and overflows.
  • River Overflow: If a river gets too full, it spills into nearby land, causing a flood.
  • Snowmelt or Glacial Melt: Sudden melting snow or glaciers adds extra water to rivers quickly.
  • Blocked Drainage: If city drains and sewers are clogged or too small, water has nowhere to go.
  • Storm Surges / Tsunamis: Cyclones or tsunamis can push huge amounts of seawater inland, causing coastal floods.

3. The Physics Behind Flooding

Water follows gravity, so it naturally moves from high ground to low ground. When the ground or river can’t hold more water, the excess spreads out over the land. Soil can absorb only a certain amount of water (saturation point), after which water runs off into rivers and streets.

4. Types of Floods

  • River Floods: Caused by rivers overflowing after heavy rain or snowmelt.
  • Flash Floods: Sudden floods caused by intense rainfall or dam failure.
  • Coastal Floods: Result from storm surges, cyclones, or tsunamis.
  • Urban Floods: Occur in cities when drains get blocked or rain is too heavy.
  • Pluvial Floods: Flooding caused by surface water accumulation, often on flat land.

5. Effects of Floods

Positive:

  • Replenishes soil nutrients in floodplains.
  • Recharges groundwater.

Negative:

  • Destroys homes, roads, and crops.
  • Causes waterborne diseases.
  • Can trigger landslides on hillsides.

6. How to Stay Safe During Floods

  • Move to higher ground.
  • Avoid walking or driving in floodwater — even shallow water can sweep you away.
  • Stay informed through news, radio, or apps.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment if wet.

In short: Floods happen when too much water accumulates faster than land or rivers can handle. Heavy rain, snowmelt, storms, or blocked drains can all trigger them. Water spreads following gravity, causing damage but sometimes also nourishing soil.
